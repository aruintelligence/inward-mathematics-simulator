# Inward Mathematics™ Interactive Lab

A dependency-free browser demonstration of two **toy models**: a damped attractor and a five-observer comparison. The equations, target values, controls, and export are visible in `index.html`. This is exploratory visualization, not a validated theory of memory, identity, consciousness, safety, or physical dynamics.

The **exact joint-uncertainty certificate** asks a second question: suppose each of the five readings can change independently by at most ε within 0–100 while the target lies anywhere in a closed interval. The squared-error difference factors into `(mean − median)(mean + median − 2×target)`. The second factor is monotone in each reading and in the target, so all-low readings with the highest target minimize it, and all-high readings with the lowest target maximize it. The exact extrema of `mean − median` come from 30 choices of a median and two readings below it. Within each ordering, pushing the other readings to their allowed limits makes this factor decrease as the median rises, so only the limiting feasible median values need checking. Integer millionth units preserve all input endpoints; factor readouts have up to seven decimals because a mean can have fifth-millionth steps. If neither factor's range includes zero, their signs prove a strict winner for **every** allowed reading configuration and every real target in the interval. If a factor spans zero, continuity proves that a tie is attainable. An endpoint tie displays explicit readings and target; an interior tie displays two allowed configurations and an exact rational fraction along their connecting path. Each reading and target at that fraction is a rational number, even if its decimal representation does not terminate on the input grid. The 2ε absolute-error bound remains available as a simpler sufficient shortcut. ## Bring your own numbers

The local workbench accepts 3–40 finite observations between −1,000,000 and 1,000,000, separated by commas, semicolons, or whitespace. An optional **known target** lets you compare absolute errors; without one, the workbench shows both estimates without declaring either more accurate. It plots the original observations, mean, median, and supplied target. It then removes the original observation farthest from the original median and recomputes both estimators. This leave-one-out comparison shows sensitivity; it does not establish that the removed point is invalid. An even-length dataset uses the average of its two middle sorted values. Ties for the farthest observation use the first entered value.

Use the invented outlier and mean-wins examples to learn the controls, or paste your own numbers. **Save this analysis as JSON** downloads the inputs, optional target, exact computed values, errors, and leave-one-out result. Analysis runs in the browser, with no application backend or analytics. The workbench does not transmit values while you edit or analyze them. If you deliberately open a challenge link, its five values and target are placed in the URL and sent in the page request. Other workbench inputs are not put into links. Display labels round to four decimal places; the saved JSON retains JavaScript's numeric values. Avoid interpreting a supplied target as independently verified ground truth.

## Experiment 1: Remembrance Calculus™

The yellow point moves toward a fixed green target. Each discrete step applies:

```text
velocity = 0.92 × (velocity + 0.008 × α × (target − position))
position = position + velocity
coherence proxy = exp(−distance to target / 120)
```

Set **pull strength α** between 0.5 and 3.0. **Step once** advances exactly one update; **Run** advances at a fixed 60 updates per simulated second. **Add shock** applies an impulse whose magnitude and direction come from an isolated seeded pseudorandom stream. The seed also generates the four fixed observer values in the second experiment. Resetting the seed and replaying shocks at the same step reproduces the same numeric trajectory. “Settled” means distance under 4 and speed under 0.5 for 45 consecutive steps; it is a display rule, not a proof of convergence under every possible input.

## Experiment 2: Witness Geometry™

Four seeded observers estimate a known target of 50; a fifth estimate is adjustable from 0 to 100. The graph and telemetry show the ordinary arithmetic mean, median, and each one's absolute error against the known target. With seed 42 and the fifth observer at 85, the mean is about **55.38** (error **5.38**) and the median about **50.12** (error **0.12**).

The sensitivity sweep holds the four seeded values fixed and computes both absolute errors for every whole-number fifth estimate from 0 through 100. The curves, current-position marker, sample table, lower-error counts, and maximum errors make the comparison inspectable beyond one selected example. With seed 42, the median has lower error at **84** grid positions and the mean at **17**; there are no ties. For example, the mean wins at 40 while the median wins at 0 and 100. These counts describe an evenly spaced grid, **not** the chance of either estimator winning on real data. Change the seed and reset to generate four new fixed values. The fifth estimate is a numeric perturbation; it does not imply dishonesty or establish a generally superior rule.

The cohort view repeats that grid for **128 consecutive generated seeds**, starting at the selected seed and wrapping modulo 2³². Each seed generates four values with four successive draws from the same seeded linear congruential generator; the fifth estimate is shared across seeds. Its curves show average absolute error across those 128 cases. The table also shows how many individual seeds yield lower median error at each sampled fifth estimate. With start seed 42 and fifth estimate 50, the mean has lower **average** absolute error (about **1.43** versus **1.56**) even though the median has lower error in **75** of 128 seeds. Frequency of winning and size of errors can disagree. At fifth estimate 85, the median has lower error in **126** of 128 seeds. The cohort is deterministic generated data; consecutive PRNG seeds are not an independent sample of real observers, and these numbers are not population estimates.

## Reproduce and inspect

The lab starts paused for everyone and respects reduced motion. Set a seed, then use **Step once** or **Run** to make an observed path.

- **See one step ahead** previews the pull term, damped velocity, next position, and distance change without advancing. A faint ring shows the next point on the trajectory while paused. Changing pull strength or adding a shock recomputes the preview.
- **Trace the path behind you** freezes up to 150 recent positions. Scrub for exact coordinates and distance, or jump to the closest captured approach, next shock, or next pull-strength change. Event jumps wrap within the frozen capture and are disabled when their only event is already selected. The distance chart marks shocks in rose and pull-strength changes in lavender; the field map locates the selected point and target, rose squares for shock positions, and lavender rings for pull-change positions. Overlapping markers show both events without hiding the selected point. Copy a link to the selected captured step to replay that moment paused with its trace open, even if the live run has since changed. The link uses the event history saved when the path was captured and carries the fifth reading from that capture; it does not record earlier fifth-reading edits. Download path CSV exports every captured step with its coordinates, distance, shock count, and pull changes. A shock changes velocity at its recorded step, while position responds on the next update. Refresh to capture later steps. A close pass alone is not sustained settling.
- **Compare two futures** forks the current position and velocity into two 60-update, no-new-shock paths that differ only in pull strength. The cursor compares any selected update; a 60-bar ribbon counts when each path is nearer, and a jump control visits lead changes. A lead at one update is not a universal ranking. **Use alternative α in live run** applies that strength at the current step and records the parameter change.
- **Make a visual run receipt** captures trajectory, seed, step, distance, speed, pull, and shock count on a 1200×630 canvas. Refresh the frozen snapshot after changes, then save a PNG named for its captured step. This image summarizes the run; the replay link or JSON carries its exact event history.
- **Export run JSON** includes model constants, seed, inputs, exact position and velocity, shocks and their impulses, parameter changes, observer values and errors. The `inward-math-toy-v3` record also includes the 101-position sensitivity sweep and 128-seed cohort protocol, generated records, aggregate points, and winner counts.

**Copy replay link** encodes seed, fifth reading, pull and shock-strength changes, shock timing, and step count. Opening it reconstructs the run and pauses for inspection. Replay links are limited to 3,600 steps and 80 changes or shocks each; they exclude workbench data. URLs can appear in browser history or hosting logs, so use invented values. The page sends no inputs to a server except the URL requested when a replay link is opened. Identical seed, changes, shock timing, and step count produce identical numeric results. Inspecting frozen traces and future previews does not advance the live model.
