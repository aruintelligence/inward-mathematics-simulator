03 / Why the boundary is exact

A finite scan covers every real drift.

Write S for the sum of the five allowed readings, M for their median, and t for any target in the selected closed interval. The squared-error difference factors as:

(|mean − t|² − |median − t|²) × 25 = (S − 5M)(S + 5M − 10t)
  1. Fix the baseline signs. At zero drift, a strict winner gives each factor a fixed nonzero sign over the entire target interval. A tie first appears when either signed factor can reach zero.
  2. Check all configurations. The target factor reaches its extremes at all-low or all-high readings and a target endpoint. For the estimate factor, five possible median indices and six choices of two lower readings give 30 ordering cells.
  3. Scan every change point. Within an ordering cell the extremal expression is affine until a reading clips at 0 or 100, or two reading intervals meet. Those finitely many landmarks include half-gaps for two moving readings and full gaps for one moving reading.
  4. Exhibit the boundary. The audit solves each affine zero as an exact fraction, chooses the earliest one, and checks that its rational witness stays inside the box and ties the two errors. Smaller boxes preserve the strict signs; larger boxes retain the witness.

This result is conditional on the five stipulated readings, target interval, and independent symmetric drift bounds. It does not establish the true target, input provenance, probabilities, or real-world estimator performance.
